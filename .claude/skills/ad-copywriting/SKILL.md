# Ad Copywriting

Copy is a filter. The cheapest lead is usually the worst lead, so every rule below is about attracting the right people, not the most.

## The inputs (get all three, or the copy is generic)

1. **Form:** 2 to 4 winning ads from the market, typed out word for word. They give hook order, pacing and close.
2. **Truth:** the business's real facts: town, services, offer, prices, how they talk. Do not infer location or offer from the ad account. The account tells you where ads were bought, not where they should be.
3. **Evidence:** what is working and what isn't in their ads now. Build on the winner; never rebuild a failure.

Say what to change from the swipes (usually the CTA: "tap the button" instead of a phone number).

## The hook

- First 3 seconds: a problem or a number. Never a greeting, a logo or "hey guys."
- Say the city out loud.
- Use a real dollar figure. Ads that name a price before the click convert clicks to leads at roughly double the rate, and the price line is the first thing production drops, so check the launched ad against the written copy.
- Put the price anchor in the first 3 seconds of on-screen text, not mid-script. Average view time is often under 10 seconds.
- Lead with the promise no competitor makes (see competitor-positioning). Never lead with what everyone advertises.

## The offer

- "Free estimate" is wallpaper. Ask what the buyer cannot learn anywhere without surrendering an appointment (usually: is this a small job or a big one?). Give that first and make the visit conditional.
- Remove the appointment explicitly in the copy: it is the actual objection.
- Explain why each form field is asked. Say a range is not a quote.
- Get the business owner's sign-off before promising anything about how they answer.

## Qualify in the ad and the form

- Name a price band in the creative. It screens before the form does.
- Every qualifying form question needs a disqualifying option ("Under $X", "Just researching"). If the lowest option equals the client's minimum, 80% pick it and the question filters nothing.
- Score creative on cost per **qualified** lead, never cost per lead.
- Stories placements buy taps, not intent. Check the placement split before blaming the form.

## Voice

- Would the owner say this standing in the customer's backyard? If it only works in an ad, cut it.
- Headings are labels ("What we build"), not taglines.
- Short sentences. No em-dash asides. No "In today's fast-paced world." No three-item rhythm lists for their own sake.
- Holiday copy says "the holidays." Never name a day or promise a finish date.
- Never claim what the owner can't back: warranty numbers, license claims, financing terms the lender didn't approve.

## Format that gets filmed

Narration over B-roll (no face on camera) is the easiest pack to actually produce: FRAME line, 4 one-sentence steps each with one clip, a TURN, then the PAYOFF line. The payoff is the whole ad; swap it and you have a new ad. Pair it with a numbered clip bank. Scripts that claim something about who the speaker is ("I turned down a job last month") die in a hired voice; mark which scripts need the owner.

## Checks before it ships

- [ ] The ad's offer appears on the landing page it points to.
- [ ] A number is in the first 3 seconds.
- [ ] Nothing here is a claim the business has not confirmed in writing.
- [ ] Reads like the owner, not like a marketing company.
