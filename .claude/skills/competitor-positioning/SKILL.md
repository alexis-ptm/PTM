# Competitor Positioning

The deliverable is one sentence: **a promise you can make that no competitor in the pull makes, plus the ads that prove the market pays for that emotion.**

Needs the output of the **ad-library-research** module (ranked ads) and the facts about the business being positioned.

## Step 1: Second feed, what they offer off-platform

Ad copy shows what they say. Their website shows what they offer. For each serious competitor pull: guarantees (and their scope), pricing, financing terms, process steps, proof claims (jobs done, reviews, years). Get real price bands for the service; specific dollar figures are the hooks that work.

## Step 2: Build the comparison table

One row per offer element. Columns: **what competitors promise | what we promise | verdict.** Then answer three questions:

1. **What is table stakes?** Whatever everyone advertises cannot be the hook. If every player says "free consultation," opening there starts a proof fight against the biggest advertiser, and a new one loses it.
2. **What is unowned?** The promise nobody makes. That is the wedge.
3. **Does the market already pay for the emotion?** An unowned position is a bet until you find a long-runner on the neighbouring emotion. With one, it is a read.

> **The wedge is never the service. It is the risk you are willing to absorb that competitors won't.**

## Rules that have each cost real money

- **Fix word order before discarding an offer.** Your offer may share a *name* with a commodity offer but not its *mechanics*. Compare mechanics. Never open on words competitors have made wallpaper; say "free" last, as a release rather than a bribe.
- **Aim for two primaries.** Find the two fears the buyer carries and answer both; let the ad platform decide which is bigger. Cheaper than guessing.
- **A swipe file is shape, not position.** Ads from other markets teach hook order and pacing. Only the local pull tells you what this market already says. Tailoring out-of-market swipes word for word reproduces the generic ad everyone runs.
- **Long-lived is not proven if it is a brand blurb.** Separate direct-response ads from brand ads before counting days.
- **Translated winners fail.** A winner in one language does not carry to another by translation; treat the new language as its own test with its own angle.
- **A zero needs a second read.** "Nobody does X" is the product only if the search was wide enough.
- **Confirm every guarantee in writing** (number and scope) before it goes on camera. A claim the business won't honor is the one mistake here that costs real money. Websites and forms often disagree with each other.
- **Financing and discount hooks win on cost per lead and lose on quality.** They select for price sensitivity. A numbered written guarantee with a penalty selects for buyers who have the money. Demote financing to the last line.

## Output

A short research doc: the comparison table, the top 5 longest-running ads with why they work, the chosen wedge, and the second primary. Then move to **ad-copywriting** and **script-pack**.
