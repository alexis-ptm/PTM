# Proven B2B hooks: selling leads to contractors

Source: hooks based on proven winners, written for David (selling qualified tree removal leads to tree service owners). The buyer here is the **business owner**, not the homeowner. Use this file when the ad sells leads, appointments or marketing to a contractor.

## What these hooks do (the patterns to reuse)

1. **Qualify by spend, not by city.** "If you're spending over $5,000 a month buying tree removal leads." The number screens out shops too small to buy, the same way a price band screens homeowners. It also tells the viewer the money is already being spent, so the pitch is a better return, not a new cost.
2. **Name the people inside their company.** Estimators, sales reps, crews, closers. Owners picture their own people when you name the role. "Your estimators still have gaps in their calendars" lands harder than "you need more leads."
3. **Concrete pictures of their week.** Monday morning calendar. Driving across town for a $200 job. Homeowners who clicked a form and stopped answering. One specific scene beats any adjective.
4. **Reframe the want.** "You don't need more tree removal leads. You need leads your sales team actually wants to call." Agree with the problem, then move it from volume to quality.
5. **Name the cycle they're stuck in.** Volume up, quality drops. Volume down, quality comes back. Three vendors to keep crews busy. Owners who have lived it recognize it in one line, and it disqualifies anyone who hasn't.
6. **Name the enemy without naming the brand.** Shared leads, racing four other contractors, the little agency sending a handful a week. Never say "Angi" in the ad.
7. **Their objection as the opening line.** "There's no way you can send qualified leads at that price." Quoting the skeptic is a pattern interrupt and handles the objection before it forms.
8. **Capacity, not need.** "Your crews can handle more work. Your sales guys can close more work." Flatters the owner (they can close) and puts the gap on lead flow, which is what you sell.
9. **Small permission close.** "Listen up." "Give me 15 seconds." The ask is tiny, so the viewer keeps watching.
10. **Risk reversal.** "You only pay for qualified homeowners." Only use it if that is the real billing model, in writing. Usually save the strongest guarantee for the end of the ad (see `ad-framework.md`): leading with it attracts guarantee-shoppers. Hook 6 is the exception to test, not the default.

## How the homeowner rules change for B2B

- No city in the hook. The trade is the filter ("tree removal," "your tree company"). Say the trade name in every hook.
- The number is monthly spend, estimates per week, or cost per lead, not a job price.
- The landing page or call must define "qualified" in plain words, or the hook is a claim you can't back.

## The 40 hooks, word for word

### Spend / ROI
1. If you're spending over $5,000 a month buying tree removal leads, I want to help you get way more jobs for that same money.
2. If you're already putting thousands a month into marketing your tree service, you shouldn't still be wondering where next week's jobs are coming from.
3. You're already spending money to generate tree removal jobs. The question is: how much of it is being wasted on homeowners who were never serious?
4. If you spend $5,000+ a month on tree leads, but your estimators still have gaps in their calendars, listen up.
5. If I could take the money you're already spending on tree leads and turn it into significantly more qualified opportunities, would you give me 15 seconds?

### Volume / Scale
6. We'll send you qualified tree removal opportunities every single day — and you only pay for qualified homeowners.
7. What would happen to your tree company if your estimators woke up every morning with a full calendar of qualified homeowners?
8. You need one thing to scale your tree removal company: more qualified homeowners who actually need trees removed.
9. Your crews can handle more work. Your sales guys can close more work. So why aren't their calendars full?
10. If your tree company could handle another 20, 30, or 40 estimates next week, listen up.
11. What if I could keep every estimator on your team busy with qualified tree removal opportunities?
12. Your tree company doesn't need another marketing agency. You need a predictable pipeline of qualified tree removal jobs.
13. If you have the crews and salespeople to take on more tree removal work, we can help solve the lead-flow problem.
14. Imagine opening your calendar Monday morning and seeing qualified tree removal opportunities booked throughout the entire week.

### Lead Quality
15. The problem with most tree removal leads isn't the price. It's that half of them should've never been called a lead in the first place.
16. If your reps are wasting half their day calling homeowners who just wanted a ballpark price, this is for you.
17. Tree removal leads aren't supposed to be homeowners who clicked a form and stopped answering their phone.
18. If you're tired of paying for tree leads who don't answer, aren't serious, or aren't ready to move forward, give me 15 seconds.
19. What's the difference between a $40 tree removal lead that makes you money and one that wastes your estimator's time? Qualification.
20. You don't need more tree removal leads. You need more tree removal leads that your sales team actually wants to call.
21. Your sales reps shouldn't have to qualify your tree removal leads for you. That should happen before the lead ever reaches them.
22. If your estimator is driving across town just to find out the homeowner was looking for a $200 job, your lead generation is broken.
23. Stop paying for homeowners who are "just curious" what their tree might cost to remove.

### Agencies / Lead Vendors
24. Every time you ask your tree lead company for more volume, does the quality suddenly fall off a cliff?
25. You increase your tree lead volume, quality drops. You cut the volume, quality comes back. Then you scale again and the exact same thing happens.
26. If you need three different lead companies just to keep your tree crews busy, there's a problem.
27. You're probably buying tree removal leads from two or three different places because none of them can give you enough consistent volume.
28. If you've already tried three or four marketing companies for your tree service and still don't have predictable lead flow, listen up.
29. Most tree marketing agencies can get you a few leads. The problem starts when you ask them to actually scale.
30. If your tree company has outgrown the little agency sending you a handful of leads every week, this is for you.

### Angi / Shared Leads
31. If you're still buying shared tree removal leads and racing four other contractors to call the homeowner first, there's a better way.
32. Every shared tree lead you buy turns into a race to the bottom on price. We built our model to do the opposite.
33. Your estimator shouldn't be competing against five other tree companies for every homeowner you pay to talk to.
34. If the homeowner has already gotten calls from four tree companies before your estimator reaches them, you're starting the sale from behind.
35. Stop paying for tree removal leads that get sold to you and five of your competitors at the exact same time.

### Pattern Interrupt / Proof
36. "Why are your tree removal leads better than the other leads we're buying?" One of our clients asked us that after we started sending them qualified homeowners.
37. "There's no way you can send qualified tree removal leads at that price." That's usually what tree companies tell us before they actually try it.
38. I'll bet on almost any established tree company watching this: if I can consistently put qualified homeowners in front of your salespeople, your team can close them.
39. The best tree companies I talk to don't have a closing problem. They have a problem getting enough qualified homeowners in front of their closers.
40. If I gave your best estimator a steady flow of qualified tree removal homeowners tomorrow, how many additional jobs could they close this month?

## Adapting to another trade

Swap the trade noun and the numbers, keep the structure. Check each swap against reality: the spend threshold, cost per lead and job size must match that trade (a $40 lead and a $200 job are tree numbers, not roofing numbers). Hooks 36 and 37 quote a client; only use them if a client actually said it.
